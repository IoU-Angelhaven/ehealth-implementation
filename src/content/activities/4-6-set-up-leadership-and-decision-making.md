Good structures give clear decision-making and responsibility at all levels, better use of resources, open reporting to stakeholders and stronger engagement from clinicians and leaders.

## Checklist

- Define who makes which decisions and who is responsible at all levels ([RACI matrix](https://www.atlassian.com/work-management/project-management/raci-chart)).
- Set up or confirm a [steering group](https://productive.io/blog/steering-committee-in-project-management/) and working groups, each with a clear task and authority.
- Write down the structure ([project charter](https://asana.com/resources/project-charter) or a similar document).
- Agree how priorities and resources are set between initiatives, to avoid duplicated work.
- Set up regular and open reporting to stakeholders.
- Define how safety and quality will be monitored over time.
- Engage clinicians and leaders and agree who leads ([stakeholder analysis](https://asana.com/resources/project-stakeholder)).
- Review the structure regularly.

## Methods and tools

- **[RACI matrix](https://www.atlassian.com/work-management/project-management/raci-chart):** Shows who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed.
- **[Steering group](https://productive.io/blog/steering-committee-in-project-management/):** A group of senior leaders that makes high-level decisions and removes barriers.
- **[Project charter](https://asana.com/resources/project-charter):** A short document that sets out goals, decision rights and what to do when problems arise.
- **[Stakeholder analysis](https://asana.com/resources/project-stakeholder):** Identifies who is affected, who has influence and what they need.

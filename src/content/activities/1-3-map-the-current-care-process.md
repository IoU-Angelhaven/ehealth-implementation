The map is also a useful source of information and ideas when you create new ways of working.

## Checklist

- Choose the care process to map based on what you are planning to implement.
- Gather the staff who work in the process and let them describe it together.
- Draw the process with all steps, handovers and decision points.
- Include the views of patients, clinical staff, administrative staff and technical systems.
- Check the map with people who work in the process every day. Look for errors and missing steps.
- Mark pain points, bottlenecks, extra work and workarounds (informal ways that staff solve problems).
- Note what works well and should be kept.
- Use the map as a starting point for the rest of the project.

## Methods and tools

- **[Process map (flowchart)](https://qi.elft.nhs.uk/resource/flow-charts):** A step-by-step drawing of how work flows.
- **[Swimlane diagram](https://lucid.co/diagram/swimlane/tutorial):** A process map with one row for each person or team, which shows who does what.
- **[Service blueprint](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/):** Shows what the patient sees and what happens behind the scenes, including staff and systems.
- **[Patient journey map](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/journey-mapping-101/):** Shows the patient’s experience from start to end.
- **[Value stream mapping](https://performanceandimprovement.nhs.wales/our-work/quality-safety-and-improvement/improvement/improvement-cymru-academy/resource-library/academy-toolkit-guides/value-stream-mapping-toolkit-guide/):** Shows where time is used and where waiting and waste occur in a care process.

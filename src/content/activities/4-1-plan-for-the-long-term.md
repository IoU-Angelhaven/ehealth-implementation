A good long-term plan covers clinical results and patient safety, staffing, legal and ethical duties, finances and digital inclusion. Without such a plan, a successful pilot can end when the project funding ends.

## Checklist

- Describe what the solution must achieve in the long term for clinical results and patient safety.
- Create a [roadmap](https://www.projectmanager.com/blog/strategic-roadmap): a long-term plan with milestones, responsible persons and decision points after the pilot.
- Plan long-term staffing: who will run, support and update the solution.
- Check legal and ethical requirements, such as data protection and patient safety rules, and plan how to follow them.
- Plan the finances: running costs and funding after the pilot.
- Create a plan to make sure that all patient groups, including those with low digital skills or accessibility needs, can take part.
- Identify benefits and risks, and plan how to follow and manage them ([benefits management](https://projectdelivery.gov.uk/teal-book/home/part-e-planning-and-control/chapter-19-benefits-management/)).
- Review the long-term plan regularly and update it when conditions change.

## Methods and tools

- **[Roadmap](https://www.projectmanager.com/blog/strategic-roadmap):** Shows the main steps, milestones and responsibilities over several years.
- **[Benefits management](https://projectdelivery.gov.uk/teal-book/home/part-e-planning-and-control/chapter-19-benefits-management/):** Identifies the expected benefits and risks, and follows them until they are achieved.
- **[Scenario analysis](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/financial-modeling/scenario-analysis/):** Describes best-case, likely and worst-case developments to support long-term decisions.

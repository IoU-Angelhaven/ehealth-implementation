It also lets the team test its ideas step by step and find risks and dependencies before implementation starts.

## Checklist

- Plan a workshop and decide who should join. Sometimes the core group is enough, sometimes key stakeholders should be there.
- Send material in advance so that participants can think about their assumptions.
- In the workshop, collect all assumptions and early ideas without judging them.
- Write each assumption as a clear sentence, for example: “We believe that [result] for [user], because [reason].”
- Sort the assumptions by risk and certainty. Which ones would make the project fail if they are wrong?
- Decide which assumptions to test first, and how.
- Write down the results and share them with the team for feedback.

## Methods and tools

- **[Assumption mapping](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-assumptions-mapping-can-focus-your-teams-on-running-experiments-that-matter):** A grid that sorts assumptions by how important they are and how much evidence you have.
- **[Liberating Structures](https://liberatingstructures.com/):** A collection of ready-made group exercises that give everyone a voice.
- **[“How might we…?” questions](https://www.designkit.org/methods/how-might-we.html):** A way to turn a problem into an open question that invites ideas.

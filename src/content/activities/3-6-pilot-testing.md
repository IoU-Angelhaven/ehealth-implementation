The focus is on following the data collection plan, solving problems quickly, writing down what is learned and creating a safe climate for honest feedback. A good pilot gives credible evidence about how well the solution works, and shows what to improve before full rollout.

## Checklist

- Recruit participants who reflect the variety of future users, such as different roles, experience and comfort with technology.
- Prepare the technical set-up and the user guides. Brief and train participants on how to use the solution and what the pilot aims to learn.
- Collect data exactly as set out in the evaluation plan.
- Follow technical performance, use and user experience, including by watching users at work ([field study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/field-studies/)).
- Hold regular review meetings with participants, fix problems quickly and keep a log of problems, workarounds and unexpected effects.
- Give support, such as a help desk, coaching and technical troubleshooting.
- Look for unwanted effects, both negative and positive.
- Study the data and report findings, lessons learned and a recommendation to continue, adjust or stop. Share them with participants and stakeholders.

## Methods and tools

- **[Pilot study](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/grants/pilot-studies-common-uses-and-misuses):** Real use of the solution in clinical practice, following the planned protocol.
- **[Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) cycles](https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement/testing-changes):** Regular review of pilot data, with adjustments during the pilot.
- **[Field study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/field-studies/):** Watching users in their normal work to understand workflows, workarounds and experience.

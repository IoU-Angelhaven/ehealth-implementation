By testing and improving in several rounds, the solution becomes more effective, easier to use and more successful in real work.

## Checklist

- Define what you want to learn and which questions you want to answer.
- Design test situations that show typical cases and difficult cases.
- Choose participants with different roles and experience, and get their informed consent.
- Choose where to test: a test room, a clinic, a home or the real workplace.
- Prepare test instructions and observation notes.
- Test with 5–8 users in each round, and write down what you see.
- Study the results, decide the most important improvements and make them.
- Test the important changes again, and write down what you learned and what you recommend.

## Methods and tools

- **[Usability testing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/):** Watch users do typical tasks with the solution.
- **[Think-aloud testing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/thinking-aloud-the-1-usability-tool/):** Users say what they think while they do the tasks.
- **[Guerrilla testing](https://maze.co/guides/usability-testing/guerrilla/):** Quick, informal tests with people in real settings.
- **[A/B testing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ab-testing/):** Compare two versions to see which works better.
- **[Heuristic evaluation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/):** Experts check the solution against well-known rules for good usability.
- **[Pilot test](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/grants/pilot-studies-common-uses-and-misuses):** A small test in real or nearly real work.

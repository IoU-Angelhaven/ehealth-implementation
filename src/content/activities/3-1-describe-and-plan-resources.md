Weak planning of resources is a common cause of delay and poor quality. A clear plan prevents bottlenecks, makes responsibilities clear and keeps the timeline realistic. Start early and update the plan as needs become clearer.

## Checklist

- List the roles needed, such as clinical leads, frontline staff, IT, project management, change management, training and vendor support.
- Describe the skills each role requires and compare them with the skills staff have today ([skills gap analysis](https://www.aihr.com/blog/skills-gap-analysis/)).
- Decide how gaps will be closed: training, recruitment or external support.
- Estimate the time each role needs in each phase: planning, pilot, rollout and settling in.
- Check staff availability, and arrange cover where clinical or operational staff are released for the project.
- Define who reports to whom, who decides what and who is responsible for what, for example with a [RACI matrix](https://www.atlassian.com/work-management/project-management/raci-chart).
- Name internal [champions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13012-020-01024-9) and super-users who will support adoption.
- Write down the resource plan, get written commitment from the leaders who release staff, and review the plan regularly.

## Methods and tools

- **[Resource planning](https://www.atlassian.com/work-management/project-management/resource-planning):** Map the roles, the number of people and the time needed in each phase.
- **[Skills gap analysis](https://www.aihr.com/blog/skills-gap-analysis/):** Compare the skills needed with the skills available to see training or recruitment needs.
- **[RACI matrix](https://www.atlassian.com/work-management/project-management/raci-chart):** Shows who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed for each task or decision.

Without it, plans stay on paper and attention shifts to other priorities. Good mobilization gives clear leadership and responsibility, builds a shared understanding among stakeholders and creates early visibility for the initiative.

## Checklist

- Get written commitments from department heads and leaders for staff, funding and executive sponsorship.
- Set up a leadership and decision structure: a [steering group](https://productive.io/blog/steering-committee-in-project-management/), working groups, a named sponsor, project management capacity and clear routes for problems that cannot be solved at lower levels.
- Secure the IT infrastructure and, where needed, start purchasing and contact with vendors.
- Agree communication channels and a regular meeting rhythm. If several sites or partners are involved, hold meetings to keep them aligned.
- Hold workshops with each stakeholder group to explain why change is needed and to gather their ideas (see [stakeholder analysis](https://asana.com/resources/project-stakeholder)).
- Hold a launch event to show commitment, and plan early quick wins to build confidence.
- Engage clinical and operational champions who can show how to use the solution and support their peers.
- Set up simple ways for frontline staff to raise problems and ideas early.

## Methods and tools

- **[Stakeholder analysis](https://asana.com/resources/project-stakeholder):** Identify who is affected, how much influence they have and what they need to know.
- **[Project charter](https://asana.com/resources/project-charter):** A short document that sets out goals, what is included, who decides and what to do when problems arise.
- **[Steering group](https://productive.io/blog/steering-committee-in-project-management/):** A group of senior leaders and sponsors that makes high-level decisions and removes barriers.
- **[Communication plan](https://www.smartsheet.com/content/project-communications-plan):** Defines key messages, audiences, channels, timing and who is responsible.
- **[Champions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13012-020-01024-9):** Respected colleagues who promote and support the change in their own teams.
